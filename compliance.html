Extensions matter more than anything else on this page. A due date extended by notification and not accounted for makes a return look late when it was not, and charges interest and late fee on days that were never delay. Two examples: April 2022 GSTR-3B moved to 24 May 2022 by Notification No. 05/2022-Central Tax after the portal failed, and September 2022 GSTR-3B to 21 October 2022 by Notification No. 21/2022-Central Tax.

3. Late fee under Section 47

Section 47(1) provides one hundred rupees per day per Act, subject to five thousand rupees. The daily rate was reduced by Notification No. 76/2018-Central Tax, and the ceiling replaced with turnover-based caps by Notification No. 19/2021-Central Tax for tax periods from June 2021 onwards.

The slabs run from the tax period, not the due date. Notification No. 19/2021-CT applies “for the tax periods June, 2021 onwards”. The May 2021 return falls due on 20 June 2021, but it is a May period — so the older ₹5,000 per Act ceiling applies to it, not the turnover cap. Getting this the wrong way round understates the fee on every pre-June-2021 period.

4. Late fee on the annual return

Section 47(2) is a separate provision with a separate ceiling: one hundred rupees per day per Act, capped at a quarter per cent of turnover in the State or Union territory — a percentage, not a flat figure. Notification No. 07/2023-Central Tax reduced both rate and cap by turnover slab, for FY 2022-23 onwards.

GSTR-9 — FY 2022-23 onwards (Notification No. 07/2023-CT)
Aggregate turnoverPer day, both Acts Cap, both Acts

When is the annual return complete? Circular No. 246/03/2025-GST dated 30 January 2025 clarifies that where FORM GSTR-9C is required, the annual return is not complete until both GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C have been furnished. The late fee therefore runs continuously from the due date to the later of the two filing dates — it is not two separate fees.

Notification No. 08/2025-Central Tax dated 23 January 2025 waived the excess late fee for financial years up to FY 2022-23 where GSTR-9C was furnished on or before 31 March 2025.

5. Interest under Section 50

SituationRateProvision

Undue or excess reduction of output tax liability24% p.a.s.50(3), pre-amendment position
Refund withheld and later found payable6% p.a.s.56
Refund ordered on appeal, delayed beyond 60 days9% p.a.proviso to s.56

Interest runs on the cash portion only. Under the proviso to Section 50(1) read with Rule 88B(1), where the return for a period is furnished after the due date, interest is payable on that portion of the tax paid by debiting the electronic cash ledger — not on what was discharged through credit already lying in the ledger. This is the single most common error in an interest computation.

Under Rule 88B(3), credit wrongly availed attracts interest only where it has been utilised; credit taken and reversed without utilisation does not.

Sections 16(5) and 16(6) are a real defence. For the four years to FY 2020-21 a demand resting on time-bar alone will not hold, and Section 16(6) restores credit for a registered person whose registration was cancelled and later revoked. Both were inserted with retrospective effect.

The conditions in Section 16(2)

  • (a) possession of a tax invoice or debit note
  • (aa) the details have been furnished by the supplier and appear in the recipient's GSTR-2B — the condition that makes GSTR-2B, not GSTR-2A, the statement to test credit against from 1 January 2022
  • (b) the goods or services have been received
  • (c) the tax has actually been paid to the Government
  • (d) the return under Section 39 has been furnished
  • second proviso — payment to the supplier within 180 days, failing which the credit is reversed under Rule 37

Section 17(5) Blocked credit — motor vehicles below the threshold, food and beverages, club membership, works contract for immovable property, goods lost or given as free samples, and the rest of the list. Table 4(B)(1) / 4(D)
Rule 86B Where taxable supply other than exempt and zero-rated exceeds ₹50 lakh in a month, at least 1% of the output tax liability must be paid in cash — subject to the exceptions in the proviso. GSTR-3B Table 6.1

Rule 42 is a computation, not a ratio applied to everything. It requires credit to be segregated first — exclusively for non-business use, exclusively for exempt supply, blocked under Section 17(5), and exclusively for taxable supply — before the common credit is arrived at and apportioned. Applying the exempt-turnover ratio to the whole of Table 4(A) overstates the reversal wherever credit is exclusively attributable to taxable supplies. It is a useful risk indicator; it is not the statutory working.

10. Which ledger pays what

LiabilityCash ledgerCredit ledger
Output tax (forward charge)YesYes
Tax under reverse chargeYes — onlyNo
InterestYes — onlyNo
Late feeYes — onlyNo
PenaltyYes — onlyNo
TDS and TCS credited to the taxpayerCredited to the cash ledger—

The last line is worth dwelling on. TDS under Section 51 and TCS under Section 52 are credited to the electronic cash ledger, not the credit ledger — so the mere existence of TDS or TCS credit can never be an excess of input tax credit. The test CBIC actually prescribes compares the taxable value declared at Table 3.1(a) against the net value on which tax was deducted or collected.

12. Limitation and penalty

Up to FY 2023-24
Section 73 — no fraudSection 74 — fraud or suppression
Notice3 months before the order6 months before the order
Order3 years from the due date of the annual return5 years from the due date of the annual return
Paid before noticeNo penalty15% penalty
Paid within 30 days of noticeNo penalty25% penalty
Paid within 30 days of order10% of tax or ₹10,000, whichever is higher50% penalty
Otherwise10% of tax or ₹10,000, whichever is higher100% penalty

From FY 2024-25, Section 74A replaces both. It provides a common limitation — 42 months from the due date of the annual return for the notice, and 12 months from the notice for the order — with the penalty still graded by whether fraud or suppression is involved. Getting the demarcation at FY 2023-24 / FY 2024-25 wrong is a common and fatal drafting error.

There is no materiality threshold in Section 61 or Rule 99. Every discrepancy is to be reported; what is worth pursuing is for the proper officer to decide.
